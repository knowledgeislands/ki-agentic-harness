# Coordinating-work standard: territory work

This file holds States of play, Project work under them, assigned packages, the owner queue, work awaiting review and the worktree sweep. It extends [the core](standards-coordination.md).

## Coordinating work

Project work coordinates exactly one Project for the owner. It is always delegation-based: it runs its work as background agents through `ki agent` under the `ki-delegation` [background-run standard](../../../governance/ki-delegation/references/standards-background-runs.md), and keeps its own turns short under [Coordinator responsiveness](standards-coordination.md#coordinator-responsiveness).

- **Work package:** the coordinator picks the work up from `+/_WORK/<territory>.prj.<project>/`, whose `work.md` defines the work and whose `state.md` holds its current state only, and keeps both current under the work-package standard. The work file names its Project in front matter, and the Project note does not link down.
- **Decisions:** it records each owner approval in `+/_WORK/<id>/decisions.md` before launching the work that approval authorises.
- **Territory work:** [territory work](#territory-work) owns cross-Project priorities, releases, and decisions touching more than one Project. Releases follow the `ki-repo-tools` release-on-demand policy: a delivery run never releases by default, and a release run carries the `release` footer for the named release only. Project work raises those there.
- **Naming:** where the runtime can name a session, the session takes its seat's thread line, such as `⚒️ 🔑 ki.prj.ways-of-working`, under [Work naming](standards-opening.md#work-naming), so territory work and the owner can find it.
- **Opening:** open Project work only where there is active work to drive, within territory work's [cap](#territory-work). A dormant, registered Project may have an On hold package solely to receive its first message, under [Seeding](#territory-work).
- **Scope:** it works only its own Project's records. Work that belongs to another Project goes to that Project's `messages.md` through territory work, the secondary State of play covering both where one does.
- **Close:** it shows Finished only at [Finishing Project work](standards-mark.md#finishing-project-work); until then it moves between the other six states.

### Territory work

Each territory Capital holds one main State of play, `+/_WORK/<territory>.sop.main/work.md`, titled `<Territory> > SoP`: the coordination of the territory's coordinations. It may also hold [secondary States of play](#secondary-states-of-play), each coordinating a named part of the territory under the main. Each does project management and may coordinate explicitly assigned Project packages: delivery remains in those packages and their product repositories, never in the State of play package itself. No repository other than a Capital holds territory work. The main is never Finished while the territory is open. Where this standard says territory work without qualification, it means the main State of play; a secondary takes those duties only for the work it covers, as its section sets.

- **Registry:** the themes and Projects it manages are defined in the Capital's `Streams/` registry under the `ki-work` Project registry standard, and their work records live in the roadmaps of the territory's repositories.
- **Owns:** cross-Project priorities, releases, decisions touching more than one Project, [messages](standards-messages.md#messages-between-work) to Project work, written into their `messages.md` and Project notes, the Project-work queue, and a territory hold with the list of work to restart from it.
- **Views:** it rolls up the five views the note sets, with you, stuck, blocked, moving and on hold, across the territory's work, through `ki work status`; work awaiting review stays out of the five views and shows as a count.
- **Regular work:** it may discuss regular work and send it messages without opening coordination on that package; dispatch follows its explicit assignment and the owner-approved scope.
- **Acting for idle work:** where a package in its territory has no active session, the State of play acts for it on its messages. A package has an active session while its `listening_status` is `on`; otherwise nothing takes its messages until it is next opened, so the State of play takes them. It takes the package's Inbound under [Messages between work](standards-messages.md#messages-between-work), IMPORTANT lines first and then in queue order, within one package per turn: it ACKs each line at the top of the sender's Inbound; routes a part that belongs to other work by a line in that work's `messages.md`, under Dispatch below; answers what the territory's coordination can answer by a reply line to the sender; and puts what needs the package's own judgement or the owner into the package's `state.md` next steps or `discussions.md`; then it removes the line in a commit naming the line and the package, `docs(work): <id> messages taken by <State of play id>`. It takes the package's work forward only where its own assignment already covers it: acting for the package, it launches no run and changes none of its state, holder, `since`, mark, hold or decisions, and an On hold package stays on hold. Acting for is not stewardship and transfers nothing under [Coordinating assigned packages](#coordinating-assigned-packages); a package whose home is absent, stale or ambiguous is surfaced, not acted for. A secondary acts for the packages it covers, the main for the rest. When the package's own session opens, it takes only the lines still in its queue and finds the dispositions in its files and Git, and the State of play stops acting for it.
- **Cap:** at most three or four Project packages are active at once, irrespective of how many threads act on them, with neither On hold nor Finished. Exceeding this existing active Project cap requires the owner's explicit agreement beforehand; the coordinator cannot raise it unilaterally. Until then the default is to queue the next piece. It keeps the active work and the ordered queue in its state file's `## Queue`, each with its title and opener, and opens the next queued piece when an active one goes on hold or finishes. Within one piece, about three background agents run at once and the rest wait in the run queue, under [Coordinator responsiveness](standards-coordination.md#coordinator-responsiveness).
- **Acceptance first:** at bootstrap and at resume, its first call on the owner is the territory's records awaiting acceptance, brought as one bundle per work under [Acceptance bundles](standards-queries.md#acceptance-bundles), before any other query.
- **Seeding:** before Project work opens, territory work creates or reconciles its work folder and gives the owner its [opener](standards-opening.md#opening-work), so the session opens with one message. The sender or the territory's State of play may create an absent, registered Project's package solely to queue its first message, within the receiving repository's authority. Create all five package files with a minimal definition, empty queries and decisions, and `state: on-hold`, `hold.reason: paused` and `hold.release: when Kris opens this Project work`; a queued message does not open or automatically release the work.
- **Flags:** at its own mark it names, by a line in each owning work's `messages.md`, every work folder the `ki-work-package` rubric's [FOLDER](../../../governance/ki-work-package/references/rubric.md) items flag under [Pruning the work folder](standards-mark.md#pruning-the-work-folder), except a finished work's folder, which it never names in a message line; it never prunes another work folder itself, except that it closes a finished work's folder directly under the `ki-work-package` [close-out](../../../governance/ki-work-package/references/standards-work-package.md#tidying-the-work-folder) and, if a close-out check fails, puts the query in its own `discussions.md`. At the same mark it runs the [worktree sweep](#worktree-sweep) and reports only what it finds.
- **Other territories:** it coordinates with another territory's territory work only through the owner. It never acts with authority in another territory's repositories; each Capital keeps its own ownership, and cross-repository work moves under `ki-trades` or the receiving repository's own process.

**Dispatch.** Territory work is the single dispatch coordinator. Every new unit of work with no clear owner - an owner dump, a handoff with no owning work, a cross-work item whose owner is unclear, and every method improvement - comes to it, by the owner pasting a prompt there or by a line in its own `messages.md`. A work's own follow-ups, an item in a record that already names its work and a reply to a message go direct and do not pass through it.

- **Matching:** it matches the unit against the register, the `work.md` and `state.md` front matter `ki work status` derives from the work folders, reading a `work.md` body only where two descriptions match, and keeps no copy of the register.
- **Routing:** it writes one dated line into the chosen work's `messages.md`, `- <date> · ROUTE · <Territory> > SoP (<territory>): <the unit in the owner's words, with links>`, at the position its priority warrants, and flags it important where it must be taken first, under [Messages between work](standards-messages.md#messages-between-work). Where nothing matches, it proposes to the owner a new piece of work, within the cap, or a record captured through `ki-next`.
- **Returns:** a part a work returns is routed once more at most; if it cannot be placed elsewhere, or would come back again, it goes to the owner as a query, so nothing bounces between works.
- **Unread lines:** it shows in its register the count and age of lines nobody has picked up. A line routed to work On hold, paused, waits without waking it; for work Snoozing whose session is closed, or not waking, it identifies the work by its thread line, and asks the owner to lift a hold where a line is urgent.

**Stewardship of continual improvement.** Every work tries to improve continually, and territory work coordinates it. Every work sends a method improvement to territory work by a `METHOD` line in its `messages.md`, and territory work watches the works for improvements itself. While Ways of working is open, territory work routes each to it like any other unit, its description saying it takes method changes, and Ways of working sends targeted `REFRESH` lines under [Refreshing this guidance](standards-coordination.md#refreshing-this-guidance). When Ways of working finishes, territory work's `work.md` description gains method stewardship and its Scope says where method changes go: it keeps a method backlog, dispatches each accepted small improvement as small regular work or into the Project it most affects, with a design review for a change of shape, and sends the `REFRESH` lines itself; a significant change revives Ways of working, or a more targeted Project under the Model theme. Lasting method authority stays in the skills and decision records, and territory work still executes nothing.

Sources: SOP-ACTS-FOR-IDLE, WORK-ROUTING and STEWARD-HOME in [the decision register](sources.md#decision-register).

### Coordinating assigned packages

The acknowledged package steward tends its disposition, queues and tidy independently of any active coordinating session. The parent State of play reconciles assigned regular umbrellas, children and declared member homes through `parent`/`covers` and the home reference at opening, bootstrap, approved moves and ordinary tending. Surface absent, stale, inaccessible or ambiguous homes and unacknowledged ownership; do not take over or clear their messages. The Capital's prose home reference is discovery, never a second package, state or inbox. Reader compatibility is a prerequisite to automated member-home discovery; the local advisory hook does not scan the estate or guarantee runtime injection.

A State of play may steward an assigned package of any level only by the explicit committed assignment and acknowledged handover in the `ki-work-package` [stewardship rule](../../../governance/ki-work-package/references/standards-work-package.md#assigned-package-stewardship). Its field remains the marked STEWARD-FIELD placeholder; do not add a field or reinterpret `worker`. The old steward stops launches and reconciles runs in flight, queued launches and pending writes; the new steward acknowledges and takes their supervision before acting. No absent session, missing binding, quiet thread or timeout transfers stewardship. Assignment never changes the holder or releases On hold work.

An assigned State of play may use the package's own entry points, including tidying. It may create or adopt a package only for a registered Project within the cap, with a state justified by the actual move. First-message seeding of dormant work stays On hold. A package assigned elsewhere receives only messages. A territory hold drains assigned Project packages first and asks any independent steward to drain its own; it does not write another steward's package.

One territory Summary reports across the packages it stewards, naming the package on every item. A query's identity is `(work id, tag)`, and its full text lives in that package's `discussions.md`; the owner's answer is recorded in that package's `decisions.md`. Each package keeps its own state, `since`, mark and beat. A territory mark never moves Project marks, and acknowledgement moves only the work it clearly names. Keep a decision spanning Projects once at the territory home and link it, rather than copying it as several grants.

A record's `project` resolves through the registry to its package and grants no authority. Selection into `now` neither opens Project work nor grants execution. Its steward adds the next-step link from the routed line at its next wake; records retain the owning repository's holds, readiness and acceptance. Project-less work goes first to its existing regular or ad hoc owner, then to the State of play's routing queue only if unresolved. Reclassification reconciles running work and report destinations first.

Every run brief distinguishes its reporting package from the product repository; the launcher homes its report in that package whoever launched it, and for an Orca worker its coordinator copies the report there at receive, the one exception the `ki-delegation` [run packet](../../../governance/ki-delegation/references/standards-background-runs.md#run-packet) names. Bootstrap and re-anchoring establish the local machine through the territory's machine inventory. Host requirements restrict device-dependent execution only: any coordinator may acknowledge, plan, ask about or dispatch a targeted line in IMPORTANT and queue order. Keep unfulfilled requirements queued and counted, flagging their age when no eligible host has taken them; never infer a takeover. Resolve concrete hosts, categories and unknown or renamed names under the `ki-work-package` [message rule](../../../governance/ki-work-package/references/standards-work-package.md#messages-lines). The session's host seat stays out of packages, holds and reports.

### Secondary States of play

A territory always has its main State of play and may add secondary ones where it has more work than one coordinator can hold, such as `HNR > SoP (main)`, `HNR > SoP (5ge)` and `HNR > SoP (sites)`. A territory with no secondary needs no change.

- **Declaration:** a secondary is territory work in the Capital, `+/_WORK/<territory>.sop.<name>/`, a five-file package at `level: territory`, titled `<Territory> > SoP (<name>)`, with `parent: <territory>` and `covers` naming its assigned same-territory Project and ad hoc work. Every package below the main names its coordinating State of play as `parent`. The main's `covers` is the register of record, including coverage through secondaries and registered work whose package is absent or pruned; secondary lists remain assigned subsets. Check parents against the register, same-territory membership, unique secondary coverage and cycles under the `ki-work-package` [definition fields](../../../governance/ki-work-package/references/standards-work-package.md#work-file). Work with no secondary stays with the main.
- **Creating:** the owner decides each secondary and its coverage at the main, which records the decision and prepares the package On hold until the owner opens it; changing a coverage is a main decision, made in both `work.md` files in one commit.
- **Front door:** the main stays the owner's only front door and the single dispatch coordinator. Owner dumps, units with no clear owner, method improvements and anything touching more than one secondary, or a secondary and uncovered work, come to the main.
- **Main owns:** the registry, membership and Known Lands, releases and priorities across secondaries, the territory-wide ceiling on active Project work, method stewardship, the worktree sweep and `REVIEW-HOLD`, and every decision touching more than one secondary.
- **Secondary owns:** for the work it covers alone, cross-Project priorities, its queue in its own `state.md` `## Queue` within its share of the main's ceiling, acceptance bundles, decisions touching more than one covered Project, and routing between them. It rolls up the five views across its covered work; the main rolls up all.
- **Routing down:** the main routes a unit within one secondary's coverage as one `ROUTE` line into that secondary's `messages.md`, and the secondary routes it on to the covered work. A unit for one named covered work may go straight there.
- **Routing up and across:** covered work sends a matter across its covered Projects to its secondary, and a `METHOD` line or any matter beyond the coverage to the main. A secondary sends cross-cutting matters to the main and may message another secondary directly where the owner of the matter is clear. A secondary returns a part it cannot place to the main, once; the main then places it or asks the owner, so nothing bounces.
- **Acceptance and review:** each secondary brings the acceptance bundles for its covered work first at its own bootstrap and resume; the main brings the rest and shows each secondary's count. `+/_WORK_REVIEW/` stays with the main.
- **Opening:** the owner opens a secondary with the [secondary opener](standards-opening.md#opening-work) and gets every opener for the territory from `ki agent prompts <territory>`.

Sources: SECONDARY-SOP in [the decision register](sources.md#decision-register).

## Owner queue

- **Owner queue:** each piece of Project work is capped at eight roadmap records awaiting acceptance and at eight open queries. Over either cap the coordinator launches no new build or planning run until the owner catches up; acceptance fixes and work that clears the backlog stay allowed. It brings the owner's acceptance as one bundle per work under [Acceptance bundles](standards-queries.md#acceptance-bundles), and the banner's counts line shows the records waiting, under [Banner](standards-coordination.md#banner). The rubric warns over either cap, under [Pruning the work folder](standards-mark.md#pruning-the-work-folder).

Sources: OWNER-QUEUE in [the decision register](sources.md#decision-register).

## Work awaiting review

`+/_WORK_REVIEW/<id>/` is the holding area for migrated work a territory's migration was not confident is live; its shape, index note, leaving rules and checks are the `ki-work-package` [Work awaiting review](../../../governance/ki-work-package/references/standards-work-package.md#work-awaiting-review) section's. For coordinating work it is not live: it is neither picked up, dispatched nor counted against the cap, and `ki work status` keeps it out of the five views and shows it as a count.

**Raising the holdings.** Territory work brings the holding area to the owner's attention. At bootstrap, at resume and at re-anchoring, territory work lists every item in its territory's `+/_WORK_REVIEW/` areas and raises them as one query, tagged `REVIEW-HOLD`, with a live-or-dead recommendation for each item: a live item moves into `+/_WORK/` as a work package under the work-package standard, and a dead item is deleted, Git keeping its history. It raises the query again each time it comes back until every area is empty. The native rubric warns while a Capital's area holds anything.

Sources: MIGRATION-REVIEW and REVIEW-SURFACE in [the decision register](sources.md#decision-register).

## Worktree sweep

At each mark territory work sweeps its territory's repositories for work left between a run and `main`, and reports only what it finds. It reads each repository's linked worktrees, branches not merged into `main`, stashes and mains ahead of their upstream, through `ki agent status --worktrees` once that exists and through read-only Git until then, and shows each finding in its banner as `- [stranded] <worktree or branch> · <owning work>`, `[branch]` or `[stash]`, whatever its age; a clean sweep adds nothing. Routine push state is omitted from the sweep report and banner. The sweep removes nothing: integrating goes to the owning work, and a stash drop, branch deletion or worktree removal goes to the owner by name under the `ki-git` [clean-down](../../../governance/ki-git/references/standards-git.md#clean-down-state) rule. It reports Git state, not age.

Sources: STALE-SWEEP and WORKTREE-START in [the decision register](sources.md#decision-register).
