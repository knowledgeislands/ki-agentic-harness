## 1. Introduction

A retrospective is the type of meeting that looks back at a meeting or a run of work for what to keep and what to change. It runs by [the meeting standard](standard-meeting.md); this standard adds only what a retrospective produces and how it gets there. No retrospective has been held under it yet; the first trial's lessons, which went into the change that set the meeting standard, are the model. The decisions behind these rules are in [the sources file](sources.md#3-decisions).

## 4. Rules

The rules, part by part.

### 4.1 What a retrospective produces

1. **What to keep and what to change,** each a lesson with the evidence for it: a message, a minute or a record.
2. **Each lesson goes to its owner:** the change document that owns that way of working, as a proposal or an open question with its recommendation. A lesson about meetings goes to the change that owns the meeting standard.
3. **No change in the meeting.** A retrospective proposes; the owner decides, and the change document carries it.

[Back to contents](#contents)

### 4.2 The brief

Beyond what every brief holds, a retrospective's says:

1. **What is looked back at:** the meeting or run of work, and its bounds in time.
2. **The evidence:** its minutes, messages, records and change documents.
3. **The ways of working in question,** and the change documents that own them.

[Back to contents](#contents)

### 4.3 Holding a retrospective

1. **Each participant gives its own lessons** first, from the evidence, then the room sorts them into keep and change.
2. **A retrospective closes** when every lesson has its owner, and the caller's closing message names each lesson and the change document it went to.
