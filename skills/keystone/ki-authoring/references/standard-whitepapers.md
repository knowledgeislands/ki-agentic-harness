## 1. Introduction

It supplies judgment about argument and evidence for an outside reader; ordinary README and knowledge-note tasks do not acquire this workflow. ## 4. Rules

The rules, part by part.

### 4.1 Purpose and argument

An industry white paper helps a defined audience understand an opportunity and make an informed decision. Establish the intended reader, their decision and the paper’s contribution before choosing headings. Build a persuasive, source-backed argument through practical learning and its implications. Persuasion depends on accurate claims: an academic methods account or an internal progress report serves a different purpose.

Help the reader understand the problem or opportunity, objectives and scope, the proposed approach, practical findings and what they mean, deployment and commercial benefits with material trade-offs, and justified future action. Let purpose determine the structure and depth; these are reader needs, not a fixed chapter template. Explain enough context and mechanism before detailed results for the reader to interpret them. Use project history and architecture selectively where they explain the contribution or a decision.

Give each section a clear reader question and connect its answer to the argument. Write positive, direct, outward-facing prose: explain what works, under which conditions, why it matters and what useful action follows. Keep limitations material and proportionate to the claim or decision they affect, avoiding repeated defensive caveats and filler. Where preceding papers or an institutional voice exist, read them to inform vocabulary, tone and the continuing argument; explain the new contribution without repeating the earlier account.

### 4.2 Evidence and presentation

Distinguish observed results, design intentions, modelled outcomes and proposed work so readers can judge maturity and applicability. Keep operating conditions and assumptions beside claims they materially constrain. Preserve neutral or adverse findings that change a deployment choice. Bound statements about missing evidence to the sources actually reviewed; do not invent results, exaggerate benefits or turn future work into a promised outcome.

Connect findings to practical deployment and commercial choices: integration effort, responsibilities, beneficiaries, costs and relevant alternatives. State benefits at the strength the evidence supports and show which next action follows. Supporting mechanisms and technical detail should make those consequences intelligible, rather than become an encyclopaedic component inventory.

Use figures to clarify a relationship, mechanism or comparison in the argument. Align labels with the prose and use coherent nomenclature throughout, defining unfamiliar terms at first substantive use and adding a glossary when useful. Keep claims traceable to their sources. Public references must identify the material actually used and be suitable for public citation; retain restricted provenance and editorial evidence gaps in supporting records. A reachable link alone does not establish public-use permission.

### 4.3 Lean authoring and review

1. Establish the audience, intended decision, purpose and relevant institutional voice or previous-paper arc.
2. Inventory available sources and their claim limits, distinguishing material read from summaries, unavailable originals and internal evidence.
3. Settle the argument and outline, then draft the body with context and mechanisms preparing the reader for results and implications.
4. Derive the conclusions from the body, then the executive summary and, where the publication format calls for it, an abstract. Each should faithfully convey the proposition, practical findings and meaning at its appropriate depth.
5. Integrate figures and references, checking that captions, terms and citations support the same argument as the prose.
6. Obtain expert substantive review of claims, coverage and implications, then line review for clarity, voice and compression; iterate as needed. Before public delivery, check citation identity, claim support and public usability.

Review restructuring, including AI-assisted restructuring, for coverage and preserved meaning: distinguish moves and compression from omissions, stronger claims or lost local qualifications. Judge the resulting argument and evidence. Human direction, source reading and expert editorial review remain substantive contributions; tool origin alone supplies neither validation nor grounds for rejection. Keep production attribution in the appropriate publication or supporting record when required by the applicable policy.

Keep authoring instructions, review issues, schedules and production history outside deliverable prose. The paper should contain justified future action for its reader; editorial reminders and unresolved internal tasks belong in working records. Review depth and iteration follow the paper’s needs, without a universal page target or review-round count.
