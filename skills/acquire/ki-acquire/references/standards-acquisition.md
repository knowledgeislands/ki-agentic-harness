# Common acquisition standard

## Scope

Acquisition faithfully preserves an authorised source scope in a temporary capture destination. Evaluation, durable receiver reconciliation, dispatch and source retirement are separate operations. A repository supplies its own capture entry, paths, request schema and publication authority. This skill accepts no configuration keys and defines no new storage layout or source adapter.

Keep simple capture simple: read the entry point and relevant route, use the available environment, preserve and verify the promised material, then report. Do not require another runtime, delegation, a roadmap item per capture or a universal request receipt for every provider record.

## Scope and coverage

Establish what was supplied, the authorised source/date window and required outputs before reading or writing beyond it. Record originating source/tool, stable source identities and dates where evidenced. Separate source chronology from capture time; unknown dates and identifiers remain unknown.

Make coverage explicit: supplied message, URL reference, bounded digest, partial source or complete export. A digest is not a raw export. A source route that requires a complete conversation, transcript or attachment inventory keeps that stronger requirement. Preserve uncertainty and the owner's explicit corrections; do not convert neutral receipt into relevance screening. A URL alone is a reference unless visiting it is authorised.

## Identities and routing

Discover existing requests, source packages and handling evidence before creating replacements. Verified retries reuse their existing identities and paths; changed scope or content has an explicit linked version or segment. An empty search alone does not establish absence. Never overwrite prior source bytes to make a retry look complete.

Request identity, source identity and package identity serve different purposes. One owner request may produce zero, one or several packages. A provider batch may preserve many source identities without one new owner receipt per item. Use the repository's established records, not another writable queue.

Route from owner/source evidence and relevant territory context. Unknown routes stay explicitly pending; conflicting routes require resolution under the source contract. Do not invent a territory or create an output merely to close a request. Record the verified request/package relationship in the established manifests and receipts.

## Preservation and originals

Preserve the material promised by the selected source contract, including source versions, provenance and explicit omissions. Keep source statements, derived digest and interpretation distinguishable. Preserve required originals as exact bytes with identity, filename, size and checksum evidence. A preview, resized rendition, descriptor or offered download does not establish receipt of an original.

A route may publish independently useful text while optional originals await consent; essential originals and existing commitments remain explicit incomplete obligations. Preserve in the established Git package or declared source store, recording external locators and hashes. Do not invent a store binding or replace earlier commitments with a later convenience rule.

## Verification and reporting

Read the promised destination back and compare the preserved text or exact byte/hash evidence. Distinguish prepared, offered, received, locally committed and remotely verified results. Claim only the level actually verified and required by the authorised route. Local commitment does not prove remote publication; a source-side marker does not prove preservation.

Report the preserved scope, destination, coverage and anything missing or blocked using the repository's one reporting contract. An unchanged verified retry is a no-op; it does not need another package. Keep capture maintenance separate so a useful capture is not delayed by speculative process improvements.

## Retained state and authority

Temporary package removal must leave the identity, version, preservation locator and handling proof needed to distinguish already handled material from a new or amended source. Provider state and owner-request receipts need not share a physical directory or schema. Do not reset or migrate state to hide a verification failure.

Capture authority does not imply permission to publish arbitrary commits, mutate a provider, reconcile a receiver, delete a source or compact history. Apply the actual repository and owner grant. Keep original memberships, source bytes, variants, recovery evidence and migration proof until their separately authorised disposition is verified. Provider-specific progress markers and retirement checks remain additional gates.
