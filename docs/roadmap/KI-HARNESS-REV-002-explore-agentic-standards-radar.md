# Explore Agentic Standards Radar

## Goal

Establish an evidence-based radar that helps Knowledge Islands notice and assess emerging agentic standards, architectural patterns, and knowledge models early enough to make deliberate adoption, rejection, or monitoring decisions.

## Context

Agentic loops, agentic trees, graph-based orchestration, graph-based knowledge, and related terms are appearing across standards work, research, frameworks, and vendor descriptions. The terms can describe materially different execution, state, memory, provenance, and knowledge structures, yet they are often discussed as though they were interchangeable or equally mature.

Existing skill REFRESH procedures track sources owned by each capability, but the harness has no cross-cutting view of emerging agentic structures or the standards beginning to form around them. A radar could preserve comparable evidence and route consequential signals to the correct capability owner without making trend-following an automatic architecture policy.

## Boundary

Do not select a preferred agent architecture, treat market language as a formal standard, automatically adopt emerging mechanisms, or duplicate the source lists and REFRESH responsibilities of existing skills. Do not assume the radar must become a standalone skill until its audience, cadence, evidence model, and owner boundaries are understood.

## Discussion

### Radar lenses

The radar should distinguish formal standards, de facto protocols, recurring architectural patterns, research claims, and vendor terminology. Candidate evidence dimensions include maturity, interoperability, adoption, reproducibility, relevance to Knowledge Islands, uncertainty, and the capability that would own any response.

### Agentic structures

The initial comparison should cover execution loops, branching or tree-shaped orchestration, graph-shaped execution, and graphs used for knowledge, state, memory, or provenance. These are separate lenses: a system can use a graph as a knowledge substrate without using graph execution, and a loop can operate inside a tree or graph.

### Capability shape

Shaping should compare a standalone radar skill with a recurring housekeeping review, a shared source registry, or composition of existing skill REFRESH modes. Any output should route to the narrowest durable owner—a source list, decision record, existing standard, or roadmap intake record—rather than becoming a second governance authority.

### Open questions

Define trustworthy source classes, review cadence, evidence-retention shape, maturity vocabulary, and the threshold at which an observed signal becomes decision or implementation work. Determine how the radar avoids duplicating research tooling while remaining useful as an ongoing governance capability.
